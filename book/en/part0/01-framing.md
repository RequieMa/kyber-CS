# L0 · Framing — What Even Is a Computer?

```
 ▶ L0  FRAMING        ◀ you are here
   L1  software
   L2  software ↔ OS
   L3  instructions
   L4  digital logic
   L5  bits & physics
```

**The question:** before we can ask how anything *works*, what *is* the thing
we're talking about?

**The idea.** Strip away the marketing and a computer is astonishingly simple to
describe:

> A computer is an **electronic device that stores data and processes it by
> following instructions.**
>
> - The **hardware** is the electronic device — the part that physically stores
>   and processes.
> - The **software** is the instructions and the data — the part that says
>   *what* to store and *how* to process it.

That's the whole split. **Hardware = the doer. Software = the plan and the
material.** A laptop with no software is a heater. Software with no hardware is a
daydream. Computing is the two meeting.

Notice this immediately reframes our donut:

- The spinning shape, the rules for rotating it, the current angle — that's all
  **software**: ideas and data.
- The screen lighting up, the chip doing the multiplication — that's
  **hardware**: the device faithfully carrying out the plan.

**The donut at this layer.** We don't open any code yet. We just sit with the
mystery. Three windows — text, blocks, dots — driven by one backend. They share
*something*, clearly, because they spin together. But what? They can't be sharing
their *appearance*; they look nothing alike. So they must be sharing something
**underneath** appearance.

**The cliffhanger.** Whatever the three faces share, it lives below "how it
looks." To find it, we have to step inside the software and ask what a program is
actually *made of*. → **L1**

> **One-breath aside.** That word *abstraction* — treating "spin the donut" as
> one idea while ignoring exactly how it's drawn — is the single most important
> idea in all of computer science. Watch it appear at every layer below.
