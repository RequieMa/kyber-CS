# Appendix C · How to Use This Book — Course Tracks

This book is a map, not a syllabus. Different readers need different paths across it.
Here are three suggested tracks.

## Track 1: "I'm Just Curious" (the 2-hour tour)

**Goal:** understand what computer science is about, without committing to studying it.

1. Read **Part 0** (all 8 chapters) in one sitting. That's the complete descent.
2. Skim **Appendix A** (CS Timeline) — the story of how we got here.
3. If you're curious about one layer, jump to that Part and read the first chapter.

**You'll walk away with:** a mental model of the full stack, from idea to electrons.
You'll know what "abstraction" means and why it's the central idea of the field.
You'll be able to place any CS topic you hear about on the L0–L5 map.

## Track 2: "I'm Starting a CS Degree" (the full read)

**Goal:** build a complete framework before your courses fill in the details.

1. Read **Part 0** before your first semester. Re-read it between semesters.
2. **Part 1** (Software) — read alongside your intro programming and data structures courses.
3. **Part 2** (Systems) — read alongside OS, networking, and databases courses.
4. **Part 3** (Hardware) — read alongside architecture and theory courses.
5. Use **Appendix D** (Further Reading) to find the right textbook for each layer.

**You'll walk away with:** a framework that connects your courses. You'll see *why*
you're taking algorithms the same semester as architecture — because they sit on
adjacent floors of the same tower.

## Track 3: "I'm Self-Taught and Want to Fill Gaps"

**Goal:** identify and fill the holes in your knowledge.

1. Read **Part 0**. Which layer made you most uncomfortable? *That's* your gap.
2. Read that Part in full. Then read the Parts below it — because understanding
   a layer means understanding what it sits on.
3. If you found L1 unclear: focus on **Part 1**, especially DSA and paradigms.
4. If you found L2 unclear: focus on **Part 2**, especially OS and networking.
5. If you found L3–L5 unclear: focus on **Part 3**, and seriously consider
   working through nand2tetris.
6. **Appendix B** (Donut Walkthrough) is your sandbox — clone the companion repo
   and experiment.

**You'll walk away with:** a systematic understanding of what you knew and what
you didn't, and a plan to fill the gaps.

## The Map vs. The Territory

A final note. This book is a *map* — simplified, abstracted, drawn so you can
hold the whole thing in your head at once. The *territory* is the thousands of
pages of textbooks, the hours of debugging, the projects that don't compile, the
systems that crash at 3 AM.

The map is not the territory. But with a good map, you'll never wonder *why*
you're walking through a particular valley — you'll know exactly where it sits
in the larger landscape, and where the next ridge leads.
